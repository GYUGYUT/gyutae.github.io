Research

What I study and why it matters.

Models that work in the lab often fail in deployment: a new hospital or camera shifts the input distribution, a modality goes missing, or the device simply can't afford the compute. My research makes vision models stay accurate as conditions change — with a particular focus on domain continual learning, where a model is updated on new data alone, without storing past data and without retraining from scratch.

01

Learning without forgetting

Rehearsal-free, prompt-based domain continual learning that adapts to new domains with a single backbone and a single forward pass.

WACV 2027 · ACCV 2026 · ICCRD 2026

02

Robust to missing inputs

Multimodal fusion that uses every modality available and still works when some are absent — one model for any input combination.

MICCAI-W 2025 · IEEE JBHI (review) · KR / PCT patent

03

Efficient by design

Lightweight conditioning, memory-efficient updates, and noise-robust representations for restoration and anomaly detection on a budget.

ACCV 2026 · ICCE-Asia 2024

Currently exploring

Continual learners that decide what to learn anew and what to reuse based on the size of a distribution shift and its conflict with existing knowledge, and efficient training — parameter-efficient tuning, distillation, and memory-light updates — so models can keep learning on a small compute budget.

WACV 2027 First author · 2025.09 – 2026.10

UniPrompt-CL: Efficient Single-Inference Continual Learning for Medical Image Classification

A lightweight domain continual learner that keeps adapting as hospitals and devices change — without storing any past data, using one backbone and one forward pass.

Problem
Prompt-based continual learning needs an extra backbone pass just to pick prompts, and per-layer prompt pools are highly redundant.
Approach
A single prompt pool shared by every ViT layer, so prompt selection and prediction happen in one pass; each new domain adds only 20% more prompts.
Outcome
Best accuracy and lowest forgetting on diabetic retinopathy, at a fraction of the training cost. Limits on heterogeneous DomainNet are reported, too.

    same-backbone method
  • 0.079average forgetting

MICCAI-W 2025 IEEE JBHI · under review First / co-first author · 2024.10 – 2026.09

Multimodal Diagnosis of Exposure Keratopathy & Infectious Keratitis

With Samsung Medical Center: diagnose corneal disease from the broad-beam photo every clinic already takes, and get more accurate whenever special-illumination photos are available. I led everything except clinical data collection — design, modeling, experiments, papers, and patents.

Problem
Slit, blue-light, and scatter images are often missing in real clinics, so models that require all four modalities are hard to deploy.
Approach
(1) Contrastively align four modalities to the broad-beam representation during training only. (2) A single ViT with mask-aware cross-attention and adaptive modality weighting.
Outcome
One deployable model that handles any combination of 1–4 images and improves with every extra photo. Evaluated on 333 patients (2,413 sets), 10 repeats.

ACCV 2026 Second author · method advisor

Spectral Descriptor Conditioning for All-in-One Image Restoration

I diagnosed that the prior 1D radial frequency descriptor loses orientation and cannot separate directional degradations such as rain and motion blur, and proposed the 2D polar extension that became the paper's core design (PFS). One parameter-free descriptor conditions the decoder across five degradations.

Patents

Applications from clinical collaboration.

  1. 2025
    Device and Method for Diagnosing Exposure Keratopathy based on Multimodal Fusion Learning
    KR 10-2025-0121342 · filed Aug 28, 2025  /  PCT PCT/KR2026/011329 · filed Aug 10, 2026

    Two-stage multimodal fusion learning framework for automatic grading of Exposure Keratopathy (EK). Trained with four ocular imaging modalities collected at a tertiary hospital (broadbeam, slit-beam, scattering, and blue-light): modality-wise features are aligned and fused during training, while inference enables modality-agnostic classification using only a single broadbeam image to implicitly leverage information from other modalities.

    Single-image inference reaches 0.841 accuracy and 0.838 F1 on four-level severity grading — an average gain of more than +16%p over a single-modality baseline, reducing acquisition burden and improving clinical applicability.
